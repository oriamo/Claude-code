ARGUMENT 02 — THE SCARCITY IS FAKE

They would rather burn it than let you afford it.

A real shortage means they run out. These brands don’t run out — they destroy the surplus on purpose, because a discount would expose the price as arbitrary.

  1. 1

    Burberry burned £0m of its own stock in one year

    £26.9m the year before, and more than £90m destroyed over five years — disclosed quietly in its own annual report. It only stopped after the public found out in 2018.

  2. 2

    Richemont bought back and disposed of ~€0m in watches

    Roughly $557m of product pulled off the market to stop it being discounted on the grey market, which would have dented the brand.

  3. 3

    Hermès makes you audition to be allowed to buy

    Customers build ‘purchase history’ before they’re offered a Birkin. That is not a market clearing a price. That is a nightclub door policy that issues receipts.

Real value survives a discount. A story doesn’t — so they burn the evidence.

Sources: Burberry FY2018 annual report / BBC / Forbes, 2018; Reuters & CNBC on Richemont buy-backs, 2018.

ARGUMENT 03 — THE CASE STUDY

Diamonds were never rare. They were rationed.

The clearest proof that a luxury price can be pure invention — because we can name the company, the ad agency, and the year.

  1. 1888

    The monopoly

    De Beers consolidates and controls roughly 80–90% of world supply for a century. Supply is throttled, not scarce.

  2. 1938

    The problem

    Depression-era sales collapse. Fewer than 1 in 10 American engagement rings has a diamond. De Beers hires N.W. Ayer.

  3. 1947

    The four words

    Copywriter Frances Gerety writes ‘A Diamond Is Forever.’ It also quietly kills resale — selling one now reads as betraying the marriage.

  4. 1980s

    The invoice

    ‘Two months’ salary’ is invented as an ad benchmark. In Japan they pushed three. No tradition. Just a number that maximised spend.

THE PROOF: LAB-GROWN

Chemically, optically, physically identical. Same 10 on the Mohs scale. Same GIA grading criteria. The wholesale index is down roughly 0% since 2018. The stone didn’t change. Only the story did.

Sources: N.W. Ayer / De Beers campaign history; Ad Age; Edahn Golan / Tenoris wholesale lab-grown index.

THE HONEST VERSION

Nobody in this room is anti-joy. Spend on food, travel, people, things you’ll actually use. But if the purchase only works when someone else notices it, the thing you bought was approval — and approval has never once appreciated.

Treating yourself once in a while is fine — the problem is that TikTok and Instagram have over-normalised it, until a haul every month reads as a personality instead of a warning sign.

  1. 01

    The dinner was never the cost

    It’s six weeks of someone’s Friday nights, held open for a thing you decided against before the starters came. You can replace $150. You cannot replace the six weeks.

  2. 02

    Attention is free to give and expensive to receive

    Keeping someone warm costs you one text a week. It costs him a whole imagined future. That asymmetry is exactly why it keeps happening — there is no bill.

  3. 03

    Same slide as the bags, honestly

    A man buys a logo to be seen a certain way. Somebody keeps a roster to feel a certain way. Both are paying for the reaction, not the thing. I’m consistent — I’m against both.

  4. ✓

    Before you come for me — yes, men run this too

    Men keep people on read for months for the exact same reason: it feels good to be wanted. Same crime, different currency, and I’ll take that L now so nobody thinks they’ve caught me.

A “no” costs you nothing and gives him his month back. A “maybe” is an invoice you’re sending someone who can’t see the total.

BONUS TAKE 02 — THE PROOF

Every one of these is a task, handed over.

SAID → MEANT

“I’m just a girl”→you handle it
“girl math”→I’ve opted out of the numbers
“I don’t do tech”→you own my passwords
“you’re better at this”→it is yours now, permanently

Say it as a joke often enough and it becomes a job description — for somebody else.

  1. 01

    The name already exists. We just won’t use it here

    Burn the rice on purpose so you never cook again and it’s weaponised incompetence. Post the identical thing with a bow and a filter and it’s a personality.

  2. 02

    ‘Girl math’ opts out of the exact thing that keeps you free

    Of all the competences to hand over as a bit, the money one is the worst possible pick. I said it a few slides ago — everyone should be financially savvy, regardless of gender. You cannot agree with that slide and then post girl math.

  3. 03

    Whoever makes the decisions holds the power

    That rigged pyramid I showed you? Women at the base, men at the top? This is the one version of it that is self-inflicted — and it gets applause.

  4. ✓

    Before you come for me — it started as a joke, and men run it too

    The bit is funny and irony is allowed. “I’m bad at texting.” “I just don’t do feelings.” “You’re better at planning.” Men have run this play for centuries with worse branding. Weaponised incompetence isn’t gendered — only the marketing is. The joke was never the problem. The joke becoming the operating system is.

Same machinery as the bags: something gets packaged as an identity, everybody adopts it at once, and nobody totals the cost until the bill is already somebody else’s.
